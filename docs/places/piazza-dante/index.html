Piazza Dante

Piazza Dante is a large public square in Naples, Italy, named after the medieval poet Dante Alighieri. The square is dominated by a 19th-century statue of Dante, sculpted by Tito Angelini.

In the book

The square at the top of the Via Roma that Lewis uses to measure the city’s mental distance from the countryside — its cafés are ten miles and a thousand years from the Bronze-Age world of Afragola. Its Café Savoia is a haunt of line-crossers, and an old woman stops him near here trying to sell her daughter.
